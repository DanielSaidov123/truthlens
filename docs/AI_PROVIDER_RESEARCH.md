This document researches options for detecting AI-generated or AI-edited video.
The research phase is complete; it does not select a final provider, and it does not
start Stage 4. The upcoming proof of concept will validate the comparative approach
and may lead to a final recommendation.

TruthLens must never present an AI result as absolute proof. `inconclusive` is used
when evidence is insufficient.

---

## 1. Problem scope

TruthLens needs to assess whether a video was **generated by AI** or **edited with AI**
and explain the basis (findings with time ranges). This is broader than face-deepfake
detection: it includes fully synthetic video, partial manipulation, inpainting, and
AI-assisted editing.

Important distinction (must not be conflated):

- **AI-generated video detection** — whole video synthesized by a generator.
- **Deepfake / face-swap detection** — manipulation of a real person's face/voice.
- **Video forensics** — detecting tampering via compression, noise, temporal inconsistencies.
- **Provenance** — C2PA content credentials, watermarks (e.g. SynthID).

These are complementary signals, not the same capability.

## 2. Candidate solutions

### 2.1 Commercial APIs

#### A. Sightengine — AI-generated video detection

- **What it does:** Detects AI-generated videos (and images) from specific generators.
  Returns a global AI probability plus per-generator confidence scores.
- **Video support:** Yes (video analysis; no live-stream on Starter plan).
- **Generated vs. edited:** Primarily **fully AI-generated** video. Per-generator
  scores act as a fingerprint of the generator. Not a general "edited real video" detector.
- **Frame/time localization:** Flags frames/time ranges where suspicious; explanations are
  opaque (score only).
- **Limits:** Starter up to 50 MB per video; Pro up to 500 MB. No live stream on Starter.
- **Latency:** Synchronous API; depends on file size.
- **Pricing (public, as of 2026):** Free tier 2,000 ops/month; Starter $29/mo (10,000 ops,
  $0.002 overage); Pro $99/mo (40,000 ops); Enterprise custom.
  Per-image deepfake detection $0.015/image via Eden AI.
- **Region:** EU hosting available.
- **Licensing/commercial use:** Commercial API, pay-as-you-go.
- **Sources:**
  - https://sightengine.com/detect-ai-generated-videos
  - https://sightengine.com/docs/ai-generated-video-detection
  - https://sightengine.com/pricing
  - https://www.edenai.co/post/best-deepfake-detection-apis-image-and-video-verification

#### B. Copyleaks — AI Video Detection API

- **What it does:** AI video detection for authenticity (generated/manipulated footage).
- **Video support:** Yes. Broad format support (mp4, avi, mov, mkv, webm, flv, wmv, mpg,
  m4v, 3gp, mxf), up to 0.5 GB and 1 hour. Async webhook delivery.
- **Generated vs. edited:** Targets AI-generated and manipulated footage (incl. synthetic
  accident/property-damage evidence, news footage).
- **Frame/time localization:** Not clearly documented as frame-level; async result.
- **Limits:** 0.5 GB / 1 hour per video; rate limit 10 req/s per account.
- **Latency:** Async (webhook) — suitable for queue/worker model.
- **Pricing (public, as of 2026):** Personal ~$13.99–$16.99/mo (100 credits); Pro ~$74.99–$99.99/mo
  (1,000 credits); Enterprise custom. 1 credit ≈ 250 words or 1 image; video credit cost not
  clearly stated.
- **Region:** Not stated in sources.
- **Licensing/commercial use:** Commercial API.
- **Sources:**
  - https://docs.copyleaks.com/concepts/products/ai-video-detection-api
  - https://docs.copyleaks.com/using-the-apis/rate-limits
  - https://copyleaks.com/pricing

#### C. Hive AI — AI-Generated & Deepfake Content Detection

- **What it does:** Multimodal detection of AI-generated/manipulated content across
  images, video, audio, text. Deepfake detection is face-focused (bounding box + confidence
  per face).
- **Video support:** Yes (video + audio).
- **Generated vs. edited:** Deepfake detection is **face manipulation** (face swap/reenactment),
  not general AI-generated video. AI-generated content classification is broader but
  documentation emphasizes synthetic media/moderation.
- **Frame/time localization:** Per-frame scores for video; face bounding boxes.
- **Limits:** Usage-based; enterprise scale (billions of calls/month). On-prem available.
- **Latency:** Real-time, high-volume.
- **Pricing (public, as of 2026):** Usage-based; ~$0.02/video (Eden AI), $0.003/image;
  Enterprise custom. Self-serve Developer tier with $50+ free credits.
- **Region:** Not stated; on-prem available.
- **Licensing/commercial use:** Commercial API.
- **Sources:**
  - https://thehive.ai/apis/ai-generated-content-classification
  - https://www.hivemoderation.com/ai-generated-content-detection
  - https://www.edenai.co/post/best-deepfake-detection-apis-image-and-video-verification

### 2.3 Video forensics and multi-signal approaches

Forensic methods detect tampering via:

- **Temporal inconsistency:** frame-to-frame coherence, motion anomalies, blending seams.
- **Compression artifacts:** double compression, DCT grid inconsistencies, quantization.
- **Noise profile:** inconsistent noise/residual profiles across regions.
- **Structural boundaries:** splicing boundaries, seam lines, resolution changes.

These methods are complementary to ML classifiers and are more robust to unknown
generators in some cases, but require specialized expertise and are not turnkey APIs.

Provenance approaches (more reliable when present):

- **C2PA / Content Credentials** — cryptographic provenance; ISO 22144 emerging standard.
- **Watermarking** — SynthID (Google) confirms Google-generated content only; absence
  does not prove authenticity.

Sources:
- https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/359969023.pdf

## 3. Critical evaluation

### 3.1 Current state of AI video detection is weak

- On the DF26 benchmark (Sep 2026, 2,691 videos: 271 real + 2,420 synthetic from
  7 modern generators — Wan2.6, Veo 3.1, Grok Imagine 1.0, Kling 3.0 (closed);
  Wan2.2-A14B, HunyuanVideo 1.5, LTX 2.3 (open) — across 3 public-speaking
  scenarios: pieces to camera, official footage, studio interviews; 1280×720):
  - State-of-the-art detectors scored AUROC 48.2–61.6 on DF26, vs. 92.3–94.3 on
    the older CelebDF++ benchmark. GenD-PE achieved the highest AUROC (69.7).
  - Human judges identified fakes only 52.6% of the time.
  - DF26 tests **fully synthetic AI-generated video** (public-speaking), not
    AI-edited real footage. It is a distribution-shift benchmark, not a measure of
    all AI manipulation types.
  Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07369v1 (arXiv:2609.07369)
- Open-source detectors lose ~half their AUC on in-the-wild 2024 deepfakes vs.
  older academic benchmarks (Deepfake-Eval-2024).
- Independent hands-on testing (MindStudio, 2026) showed commercial detection APIs
  and LLM-based video understanding frequently disagreed and misclassified obvious
  AI videos as "probably not AI."
  Source: https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/ai-detection-tools-accuracy

Conclusion: **no single detector is reliable today.** TruthLens must treat results as
evidence-based assessments, use `inconclusive` when evidence is insufficient, and
never claim 100% accuracy.

### 3.2 Deepfake ≠ AI-generated video

- Hive's deepfake detection is **face-focused** (face swap/reenactment). It does
  not detect fully synthetic non-face video or general AI editing.
- Sightengine and Copyleaks target AI-generated video more broadly, but still
  rely on trained-generator fingerprints.
- Conflating these would overstate capability and mislead users.

### 3.3 Generalization to new generators is poor

- Detectors trained on specific generators degrade on unseen/newer models.
- DF26 shows severe drops on generators released Jul 2025–Mar 2026.
- No vendor publishes reliable accuracy on unseen generators; vendor claims
  (e.g. "99% accuracy") must be independently verified.

### 3.4 Compression, resizing, editing degrade detection

- Compression and resolution changes destroy fine-grained artifacts.
- Structural boundaries (blending seams) are more robust than texture details.
- Edited real video (partial AI manipulation) is harder than fully synthetic video.

### 3.5 False positives on real footage

- Real footage can be flagged (e.g. heavy compression, unusual lighting).
- User-facing language must be cautious ("likely", "possible", "inconclusive").

## 5. Recommendation for PoC

### 5.1 Approach: comparative evaluation (not a single vendor choice)

Do **not** commit to a single provider based on marketing. Run a controlled PoC
comparing **Sightengine** and **Copyleaks** (and **Hive** for face-deepfake cases)
on the same labeled set, then decide.

Rationale for including both:

- **Sightengine** is self-serve, transparently priced, returns per-generator scores
  and frame-level time ranges, and supports video. It is the natural baseline.
- **Copyleaks** supports longer/larger videos (0.5 GB / 1 h) and async webhook
  delivery, which fits the worker/queue model; it also claims audio detection and
  partial (hybrid) AI footage. Its video credit cost must be verified before
  committing.
- **Hive** is a complementary signal for face deepfakes and audio, not a primary
  general AI-video detector.

### 5.2 PoC test set

Build a labeled set of at least the following (sizes are planning figures, not
claims):

1. **Real footage** (≥ 50 clips): diverse sources, resolutions, compression levels,
   including heavy compression and re-encoding.
2. **Fully AI-generated video** (≥ 50 clips): from ≥ 3 generators (e.g. Sora, Veo,
   Kling, Runway, open models), varied scenes and durations.
3. **AI-edited real footage** (≥ 30 clips): real video with partial AI manipulation
   (inpainting, object replacement, face swap) — to test edited-vs-generated.
4. **Compression/resolution variants** of the above (re-encode, resize, crop) to
   measure robustness.
5. **Mixed-content clips** (real + AI segments) to test time-range localization.

### 5.3 Metrics to measure

- Precision, recall, F1 on fully AI-generated video.
- False-positive rate on real footage (target ≤ 10%).
- AUROC on the labeled set.
- Time-range localization accuracy (IoU of detected vs. actual AI segments).
- Latency per video (p50/p95) and cost per video.
- Behaviour on compressed/resized variants (degradation).

### 5.4 Decision criteria

- **Adopt a provider** if: AUROC ≥ 0.75 on fully AI-generated video, false-positive
  rate ≤ 10% on real footage, and time-range output is available.
- **Prefer Sightengine** if: self-serve pricing fits, file limits (50/500 MB) are
  acceptable, and frame-level scores are usable.
- **Prefer Copyleaks** if: longer/larger videos are needed, async webhook fits the
  worker model better, or audio detection is required.
- **Add Hive as a secondary signal** if face-deepfake cases are common.
- **Fall back to multi-signal + human review** if no provider meets the bar: combine
  forensic signals (temporal inconsistency, compression artifacts) and provenance
  (C2PA/SynthID) and mark results `inconclusive` when evidence is weak.
- **Switch providers** if: accuracy below bar, new generator not detected and vendor
  cannot update, pricing/limits do not fit, or explainability (frame-level reasons)
  is required.

### 5.5 Caveats

- Vendor accuracy claims (e.g. Sightengine "98.3%") come from vendor-run benchmarks
  on images, not independent video evaluation. They must not be treated as fact.
- Sightengine returns opaque scores (no human-readable rationale).
- Copyleaks credit cost for video is not clearly documented; verify before committing.
- No provider is known to reliably detect partial AI edits in real footage.

## 6. Alignment with ARCHITECTURE.md

- **Provider independence:** The result contract (`verdict`, `findings`, `provider`,
  `model`) already supports swapping providers. `provider = "mock"` in Stage 3 is
  never presented as real.
- **No new infrastructure:** Commercial API fits the existing worker (HTTP call from
  worker to provider). No external queue or new service needed.
- **Result contract:** The KAN-15 result contract defines verdicts
  (`likely_ai_generated`, `possible_ai_edit`, `no_clear_ai_evidence`, `inconclusive`),
  findings (`type`, `startSec`, `endSec`, `description`, `confidence`, `evidence`,
  `severity`), confidence and result-level fields (`summary`, `limitations`). Time
  ranges support frame-level localization when the provider returns them.
- **Cautious honesty:** `inconclusive` is used when evidence is insufficient, which
  aligns with the weak state of current detection.

## 7. Open questions

1. Which provider (Sightengine vs. Copyleaks) wins on the evaluation set — must be
   tested, not assumed.
2. Whether frame-level localization is available and reliable from the chosen API.
3. Whether the product should expose a "likely" vs. "detected" distinction to users.
4. Cost at scale (video minutes, not just request count).
5. Legal/privacy implications of sending user videos to a third-party API (KAN-16).
